# Atlas Guide

https://youtu.be/8A56yWDgX0w

## 0:00–0:15 — Introduction

Welcome to Atlas, an interactive three-dimensional anatomy explorer. It lets you inspect complete reference models, search named structures, peel away layers, and open focused views.

## 0:15–0:35 — Models and orientation

The model selector switches between detailed male, full-resolution male, standard male, female, embryo, and cell references. Orbit around the model, zoom into a region, or choose a front, back, side, above, or below view.

## 0:35–0:50 — Systems, regions, and depth

The left panel groups anatomy by system. Toggle systems such as muscles, vessels, skeleton, or organs. Regions narrows the view to a body area, while Depth peels through the body from skin toward deeper structures.

## 0:50–1:05 — Hierarchies, including Drugs

Additional hierarchies reorganize the same anatomy for different learning tasks. Open the hierarchy menu and choose Drugs to browse medication-related categories mapped onto the body. Search within the Drugs hierarchy, expand a category such as Alimentary tract and metabolism, and toggle its visibility to compare the group with the surrounding anatomy. Collapse it again to return to the overview, then switch back to Systems or another hierarchy.

## 1:05–1:25 — Search and inspection

Search by an anatomical name or atlas identifier. Select a result to highlight it, focus the camera, and open its detail card. The card provides the structure’s hierarchy and educational description. Multiple results can be added to a selection set.

## 1:25–1:50 — What the item details contain

The item detail panel gives the full anatomical path, from the whole body through its system, tract, region, and local group. It explains what the structure is and includes related structures, such as what comes before it, what follows it, and its innervation and blood supply. Scroll further to see peel depth, reversible layer controls, and identifiers from TA98, THA, FMA, Latin terminology, and Atlas IDs. Use the selected-structure controls to center it, show it by itself, show related structures together, or hide it.

## 1:50–2:15 — Isolation and exploded anatomy

Study tools keep selected structures solid while the surrounding tissue becomes transparent. Isolate the selection to remove context, or use Explode to spread visible anatomy into a readable inventory of its component pieces.

## 2:15–2:35 — Cross-sections and views

Sections add axial, sagittal, coronal, or oblique cuts. Move the cut through the model and use two section tabs when comparing planes. Share and save stores a named view in the browser and generates a self-contained URL.

## 2:35–2:55 — Export and collaboration

Download a labeled PNG or record the current view as an MP4 video. Atlas also supports shareable embeds and live host-and-guest sessions. The MCP tools let compatible assistants search anatomy and open the same focused viewer.

Atlas is an educational reference for exploring anatomy, not a diagnostic or surgical tool.
